**I have spent today establishing that the spaceship computers I have admired since I was a child would be completely useless to actually own.**

Context, if you missed yesterday: I am building a status page for my house, and I have decided it ought to look like a Stargate console, for reasons that would not survive any sort of scrutiny.

So this morning I went and looked at one properly. Not fondly. Professionally, with every intention of stealing from it.

They're terrible. I'm sorry. They are genuinely terrible.

Everything is enormous. There are about four pieces of actual information on the entire screen. Half the elements are moving and don't mean anything, because movement reads well on camera and stillness doesn't. The whole thing is built to be legible to a lens for four seconds while somebody stands in front of it saying something important, and it has never once had to tell you eleven things at the same time, because no scene has ever needed it to.

Whereas a status page has to tell you eleven things at the same time, mostly in order to say that all eleven of them are fine.

So the rule for the rest of the day became: it should feel like the thing, not be the thing. Take the colours. Take the chunky edges. Absolutely take the typeface, that was forty minutes of my one life and I intend to get my money's worth. But let the layout answer to the hallway rather than the director.

And then, having done all of that very cleverly, I put it on the screen in the hall and walked past it and could not read a single word.

I had built the entire thing sat at a desk with my face about sixty centimetres from a monitor. It lives in a corridor where people see it from three metres away, at an angle, while walking, usually carrying something in both hands.

Everything was too small. The contrast was perfectly fine at a desk and hopeless in a hallway with a window at one end. The most useful sentence on the whole page was sat exactly where your eye arrives last.

There is no clever technique for this. You go and stand where the person stands. That is the entire method, and apparently I need to be taught it again from scratch every single time.

Rebuilt for three metres. It's uglier and enormously better.

The typeface survived. I'd like that noted.

Right. New Prime Minister tomorrow. Back to the real world in the morning.
