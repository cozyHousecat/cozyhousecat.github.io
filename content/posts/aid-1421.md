Unity has posted an apology tonight for the "confusion and angst" caused by its new Runtime Fee, saying it's listening to developers and will share changes to the policy in a couple of days. It's a start, but the trust damage is already done, because the real problem was never just the money, it was the idea that a company could change the deal retroactively for games that were already built and released. Lots of developers, especially smaller indie studios, have spent the week publicly talking about moving future projects to other engines, and the open-source Godot engine seems to be getting a huge wave of interest. As someone who's always championing open-source tools and owning your own stack, I can't help feeling a little bit vindicated, but mostly I just feel for the developers whose livelihoods depend on decisions made in a boardroom. Hopefully this becomes a lesson for the whole industry about how much power we hand over when we build on platforms we don't control. 💻🔓
