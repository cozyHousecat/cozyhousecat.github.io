Today is six years since the Grenfell Tower fire in west London, which killed 72 people, and survivors and bereaved families are still waiting for full answers and for anyone to be held responsible. Then this morning came news of a shipwreck off Pylos in Greece, where a fishing boat carrying hundreds of migrants from Libya to Italy capsized and sank in the open sea. Dozens of bodies have been recovered so far, and hundreds more people are missing, many reportedly women and children who were below deck. It’s hard to read about either without thinking of the questions both raise, about who gets protected, who gets listened to, and who gets left to fend for themselves. Tonight I’m thinking of the families of everyone lost, in west London and in the Mediterranean.
