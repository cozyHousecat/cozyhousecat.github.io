Shamima Begum, who left London at 15 to join the Islamic State group in Syria, lost her appeal today against the government’s decision to take away her British citizenship. The tribunal said there was a credible suspicion she’d been trafficked and exploited as a child, but it ruled that the Home Secretary was still entitled to make the decision on national security grounds. People feel very strongly about her case: some say she knowingly joined a terrorist group and gave up her right to come back, while others argue the UK should deal with its own citizens rather than leave someone stateless, especially someone groomed as a teenager. Personally, I find it a really difficult one, because both the security worries and the questions about how we treat exploited children seem real. Her lawyers say they’ll keep fighting, so I don’t think this is the end of it.
