The Online Safety Bill has passed its final stage in Parliament today and is now on its way to becoming law, making Ofcom the regulator for big tech platforms, with fines of up to £18 million or 10% of global turnover for companies that don't protect users, especially children, from harmful content. There's a lot in it that I genuinely support, because platforms have spent years putting engagement ahead of safety, and many parents and campaigners who have lost children to online harms have fought incredibly hard for this. But the bit that still worries me as a privacy and open-source person is the power that could, in theory, require messaging services to scan people's private messages, which experts say isn't possible without undermining end-to-end encryption for everyone. The government said earlier this month that Ofcom would only use that power when it's "technically feasible", which calmed things a bit, but the power itself is still there, and services like WhatsApp and Signal have previously said they'd rather leave the UK than weaken encryption. I really hope the way it's implemented manages to protect children without quietly weakening the security that keeps all of us safe, because those two things shouldn't have to be in conflict.
