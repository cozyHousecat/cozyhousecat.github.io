Europe Calls Out Big Tech

2023-10-11cozyHousecat

The humanitarian situation in Gaza seems to be getting worse by the hour, with its only power station shutting down today after running out of fuel, so hospitals are now relying on generators, and I’m thinking of everyone there and in Israel tonight. Alongside the conflict itself, the misinformation problem I wrote about at the weekend has turned into a real test of the EU’s new Digital Services Act, with EU commissioner Thierry Breton sending stern letters to Elon Musk yesterday and Mark Zuckerberg today, giving them twenty-four hours to explain how they’re tackling illegal content and disinformation on X and Facebook. X’s CEO Linda Yaccarino has replied saying the platform has removed or labelled tens of thousands of posts, while Musk has asked the EU to list the violations publicly, so it’s already turning into a bit of a standoff. I’ve got mixed feelings about it, because I strongly believe platforms should take responsibility for what they amplify, but I also think we need to be careful about how much power governments have over what can be said online. Done well, though, this could be the moment the DSA shows it has real teeth, and given how much harm false information can cause at a time like this, I think that matters a lot. 🇪🇺📱
