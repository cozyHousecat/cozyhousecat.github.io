When the Flight Plans Stop

2023-08-28cozyHousecat

It turns out the problem was in NATS’s flight planning system, which stopped processing flight plans automatically, so controllers had to enter them by hand and limit how many aircraft could fly, and over a thousand flights have been cancelled even though they said it was fixed by mid-afternoon. As someone who keeps systems up for a living, I’ve got a lot of sympathy for whoever was on call today, because there’s no worse feeling than watching something critical fall over on a bank holiday while the whole country notices. The bit that makes me twitchy is that the fix doesn’t end the pain: planes and crews are now in the wrong places, and the airlines are warning the knock-on effects could last for days. I’ll be very interested to read the explanation of what actually went wrong, and why the fallback was “do it manually, slowly” for something this important. Mostly, though, I feel for the families who’ve had their holidays ruined, and the airport staff dealing with very tired, very cross people tonight. 💙
