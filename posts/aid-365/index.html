Six acres

2022-09-25 2 min read (333 words)

There’s a new island in the Pacific. An underwater volcano near Tonga has been busy and has put up a fresh bit of land that started at about an acre and got itself to six inside a week, which is a faster rate of growth than anything else I can think of, including my nails, which have been my only real metric for growth this month.

The people who know about these things say it will very probably be gone again before long, because it’s made of the sort of loose stuff the sea takes back, and this apparently happens rather a lot and most of them don’t last.

I have been thinking about it all day in a way that is slightly out of proportion to it being, essentially, a gravel bank a very long way away.

I spent yesterday on backups, and I have spent most of this month on making things survive things, and quite a lot of the month before that on the same, and the whole shape of my thinking at the moment is how do I stop this going away, what happens if this goes away, who can get this back if it goes away and I’m not here to be asked. Which is a fine way to run a house full of computers and a fairly exhausting way to look at the world.

And then here’s an island that’s just going to be an island for a bit, doing nothing in particular, unvisited, six acres of new ground with no name and no plans, and it is going to sink back into the sea and nobody is going to write it up as an incident.

I don’t have a lesson about it. I’ve just liked it being there today, and I’ll like it having gone, and I think that’s allowed to be the whole thing.

Back to work tomorrow. Roast was good. The tin is soaking and will be soaking until roughly Wednesday, as is traditional.
