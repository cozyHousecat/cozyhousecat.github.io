The boss of NATS has said tonight that yesterday's air traffic control failure was caused by incorrect flight data the system received, not a cyber attack, and that it was the automatic safety measures shutting things down to stop bad information reaching controllers. As a fellow IT person I find that both reassuring and a bit alarming, because it suggests the system did what it was designed to do and failed safe, but also that one bad bit of data could ground half the country's flights on a bank holiday. There are still hundreds of cancellations today while airlines try to get planes and crews back where they need to be, so the knock-on chaos is far from over. I'm really hoping the full report talks openly about how the input was validated and why the backup didn't simply carry on, because that's the kind of lesson every infrastructure team, mine included, can learn from. In the meantime, it's a good reminder to double-check what I'm feeding into my own systems at work tomorrow. 💻✈️
