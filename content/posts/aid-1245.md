Two days after Wagner’s march towards Moscow, Prigozhin released his first audio message since, insisting it was a protest about how his fighters were being treated, not an attempt to overthrow the government. Tonight Putin gave a TV address praising Russians for staying united, and offering Wagner fighters a choice: join the regular army, go home, or follow Prigozhin to Belarus. He also said the organisers of the mutiny had betrayed their country, which is quite a thing to say about someone he’s just let walk away. It’s still very unclear what deal was made, and whether either man is as secure as he’s trying to look. The whole thing has been a reminder that, from the outside, we often have very little idea of what’s really happening inside the Kremlin.
