Rapid Reset

2023-10-10cozyHousecat

Today was a big day in the world of infrastructure security, with Google, Cloudflare and Amazon Web Services all revealing that they’ve been fighting off the largest denial-of-service attacks ever recorded, using a newly disclosed weakness in the HTTP/2 protocol that’s being called “Rapid Reset”. The trick is that attackers open a request and immediately cancel it, over and over again at enormous speed, which forces servers to do lots of work while costing the attacker very little, and Google says one attack peaked at a mind-boggling 398 million requests per second. What I find genuinely impressive is that the big providers quietly worked together to mitigate the attacks and coordinate patches before going public, so fixes are already rolling out, including in Microsoft’s Patch Tuesday updates today. It’s also a good reminder that the problem lies in the protocol itself, rather than in any single product, so pretty much anyone running a web server, load balancer or reverse proxy should be checking for updates this week. My to-do list at work just got a little bit longer, but honestly, I love it when the security community shows how collaboration can make the whole internet safer. 🛡️💻
