This year's Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine has gone to Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman for the discoveries that made mRNA vaccines like the COVID-19 jabs possible, and I think it's one of the most deserved prizes in years. What I love most about the story is Karikó herself, because she spent decades working on mRNA while being turned down for grants, demoted, and told the idea would never work, and she just kept going anyway. Their breakthrough was working out how to tweak the mRNA so the body's immune system didn't attack it before it could do its job, which sounds small, but it ended up helping protect billions of people during the pandemic. It's a lovely reminder that the most important science often looks like a dead end for years before it suddenly changes the world, and that we should keep funding people who are willing to stick with an unfashionable idea.
