Rishi Sunak gave a speech this afternoon announcing a big change to the UK's approach to net zero, pushing the ban on new petrol and diesel cars back from 2030 to 2035, and easing the plans to phase out gas boilers in homes. He says the UK is still committed to reaching net zero by 2050, and argues that this is a more "pragmatic" path that won't force costly changes on families during a cost of living crisis, and he's also raised the grant for heat pumps to £7,500. Critics, including some car makers like Ford who have already invested billions in electric vehicles, say the U-turn creates uncertainty for businesses and could cost the country jobs and investment, while some of the other measures he said he was scrapping, like taxes on flying or meat, were never actually government policy. Personally, I can understand worries about the cost of upgrades for ordinary households, but I also think stability matters a lot when people and businesses are making long-term plans, and changing the goalposts this late feels like it could make the transition harder, not easier. Some slightly better news on the money side, though: inflation unexpectedly dropped to 6.7% today, which hopefully means a bit of breathing room for everyone's budgets. 🌍🚗
