Russia held its annual Victory Day parade in Red Square today, marking the defeat of Nazi Germany, but it was noticeably smaller than usual, with just one old tank rolling through. In his speech, Putin claimed the West had started “a real war” against Russia, which is hard to square with the fact that Russia invaded its neighbour. Ukraine marked today as Europe Day instead, a deliberate choice by Zelensky to move away from Soviet traditions and towards Europe, with Ukraine now remembering the end of the Second World War on 8 May like much of Europe. It’s striking how the same day, marking the same victory, now means such different things in the two countries. History is always being used to tell stories about the present, and today was a very clear example.
