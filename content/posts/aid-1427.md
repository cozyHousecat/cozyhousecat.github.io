After ten days of fury from developers, Unity has published a revised version of its Runtime Fee, and it's a big climbdown from the original plan. The fee won't apply to Unity Personal at all, it'll only kick in for games made with next year's version of the engine rather than games already out, and studios that do qualify can choose to pay either 2.5% of revenue or a fee based on new players, whichever is lower. It's a much fairer deal, especially for indie and hobby developers, and the fact it's no longer retroactive is the most important change of all, because that was what made people feel the rug had been pulled out from under them. Even so, I don't think the damage disappears overnight, and plenty of studios have now seen that their engine provider can change the terms whenever it likes, so I expect the interest in open-source alternatives like Godot to stick around. It's a good reminder that community pushback really can work, and that owning more of your own stack is always worth thinking about. 💻🔓
