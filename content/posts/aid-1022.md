Today marks 25 years since the Good Friday Agreement was signed, bringing an end to most of the violence of the Troubles in Northern Ireland, which killed more than 3,500 people over three decades. I was only a child in 1998, so it’s hard to imagine how it felt at the time, but generations have since grown up in a far more peaceful Northern Ireland because of it. It hasn’t been perfect: the power-sharing government at Stormont still isn’t sitting, and today there was trouble in Derry, with petrol bombs thrown at police during an unofficial parade. Joe Biden arrives in Belfast tomorrow to mark the anniversary, and many people hope it will help push the parties back into government. Whatever the problems now, it’s worth remembering just how much courage it took for people on all sides to choose peace.
