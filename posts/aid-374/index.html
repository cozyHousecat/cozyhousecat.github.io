It hit

2022-09-27 2 min read (235 words)

So it hit. Went straight into the middle of it, and the last full picture is a proper landscape with boulders on it, a place, seven million miles away, that no human being had ever seen before and which we saw for about one second before putting a vending machine through it at fourteen thousand miles an hour.

I have watched the last few frames back an embarrassing number of times this morning. You can see individual rocks. You could pick one up.

The bit I’ve enjoyed most today, though, is finding out that nobody yet knows whether it worked.

They know it hit, which was the enormously difficult part and which is done. Whether the orbit actually moved is a completely separate question, and answering it means people sitting at telescopes on the ground for the next few weeks, timing how long the little one takes to go round the big one, and comparing that to how long it used to take. It’s a stopwatch, essentially. After all that, the answer arrives by stopwatch.

I find this incredibly comforting for reasons I can’t entirely defend. Something about the fact that the dramatic bit is over and the part that decides whether it counts is a few people patiently measuring the same thing again and again for a month.

Running on about four hours’ sleep and I have a meeting at two. Absolutely worth it.
