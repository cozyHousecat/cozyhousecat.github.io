What a day. After taking over Rostov, a Wagner convoy headed north towards Moscow, getting within a couple of hundred kilometres of the capital, while Russian troops set up roadblocks and the city declared emergency measures. Then this evening, out of nowhere, Prigozhin announced he was turning his fighters around to avoid bloodshed, after a deal brokered by Belarus’s president, Alexander Lukashenko, under which Prigozhin will apparently go to Belarus and the charges against him will be dropped. Nobody really knows what happened behind the scenes, but it’s the biggest challenge to Putin’s power in the 23 years he’s been in charge, and it showed how fragile things might be inside Russia. Whether it changes anything in Ukraine is anyone’s guess, but it’s been one of the most surreal days of news I can remember.
