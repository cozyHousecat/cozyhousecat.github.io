Baroness Louise Casey’s review of the Metropolitan Police, published today, found Britain’s biggest force to be institutionally racist, misogynistic and homophobic. It describes a culture where bullying and discrimination went unchallenged, where women in the force and the public were failed again and again, and where officers like Wayne Couzens and David Carrick slipped through. Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley accepted the findings and apologised, but said he didn’t want to use the word “institutional” because people understand it in different ways, which some people felt missed the point. Casey warned that if the Met doesn’t change, it could be broken up, which is an extraordinary thing to say about any police force. After everything we’ve learned this year, from Sarah Everard to Carrick, this feels like the moment it has to change, because people need to be able to trust the police who are meant to protect them.
