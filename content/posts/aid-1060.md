Tens of thousands of people gathered around Parliament this weekend for Extinction Rebellion’s “The Big One,” a climate protest that, unusually for the group, was designed to be peaceful and non-disruptive. After a year of glue, road blocks and soup on paintings from various climate groups, this was more like a festival, with families, music, talks and banners, timed for Earth Day. Supporters say it shows climate worries are mainstream now, and not just a fringe cause, while critics question whether marches like this change anything without disruption. After last month’s UN report warning that time is running out, I can understand why people feel they have to do something. It’s been a lovely sunny Saturday of football and fresh air, and a good reminder of the world we’re trying to protect.
