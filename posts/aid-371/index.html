Hitting it on purpose

2022-09-26 2 min read (277 words)

I am up, I have tea, the stream has been on for three quarters of an hour, and in about twenty five minutes a spacecraft roughly the size of a large vending machine is going to hit an asteroid on purpose at something like fourteen thousand miles an hour.

The asteroid is called Dimorphos, it is not coming for us, it has never been coming for us, and that’s rather the point. The whole exercise is to find out whether you can nudge one of these things off course by hitting it, and the sensible way to find that out is on a rock where getting it wrong doesn’t matter, well before there’s a rock where it does.

The camera on the front sends pictures back the whole way in and then stops, for reasons that become obvious about a second later.

That is the bit I’ve been thinking about all evening. There’s a little grey dot that gets bigger and bigger and turns into an actual place with boulders on it that nobody has ever seen before, at which point we will have both discovered it and destroyed our only means of looking at it, inside about five minutes, which has to be some sort of record.

I don’t have anything clever to say about it. I just think it’s tremendous that a fairly large number of people spent years on a thing whose entire purpose is to find out, in advance, calmly, whether an idea works, so that nobody ever has to find out in a hurry.

Anyway. Twenty minutes. I’m going to be absolutely useless tomorrow and it’s worth it.

Right, here we go.
