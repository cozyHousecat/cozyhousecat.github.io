They had to close the queue today because it got too full, which is a sentence I don't believe anybody has ever previously had cause to write down.

It's been going since Wednesday. Miles of it along the river, and people have been standing in it for something like fourteen hours at the worst of it, overnight, in September, wrapped in those foil blankets they hand out at the end of a marathon.

I've been watching the tracker rather than the queue, if I'm honest. There's a live stream showing where the back of it currently is, and a page telling you how long you'd be in for if you set off now, and I have checked that page considerably more often this week than any woman with no intention of going to London has any business doing.

I'm not going to go. I've thought about it properly and it isn't mine to go and do, and I'd be going for the queue rather than for the thing at the end of the queue, which doesn't strike me as a good enough reason to take somebody else's place in it.

I do understand it completely though. Somebody has handed this country a thing that can actually be done, with a beginning and a middle and an end and a clear price in hours, at a point where nothing else on offer to anybody has had any of that about it. Of course there's a queue. Standing in a line for fourteen hours is something a person can get up and do, whereas the rest of this fortnight has been sitting down and watching other people do things on the telly.

Season ends tomorrow and I've got eleven levels left, which has been the other great national emergency round here this week.

Priorities in excellent order, as ever.
