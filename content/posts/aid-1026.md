Joe Biden landed in Belfast tonight to mark 25 years of the Good Friday Agreement, and he’ll meet Sunak tomorrow before heading south to Ireland, where his family has roots. He’s expected to encourage Northern Ireland’s parties to get Stormont working again, which a lot of people there hope will happen. Meanwhile, the BBC got a surprise interview with Elon Musk at Twitter’s headquarters, where he admitted running Twitter has been “quite painful,” said he sometimes sleeps in the office, and said he’d sell the company for the right price. It came days after Twitter labelled the BBC as “government-funded media,” which the BBC objected to because it’s paid for by the licence fee, not the government. It was a strange mix of history and chaos for a Tuesday night.
