Meta released Llama 2 today, a powerful AI language model that anyone can download, run on their own hardware and even use in commercial products, with Microsoft as a launch partner. As someone who cares a lot about open source and owning my own tech, I find this genuinely exciting, because it means people don’t have to send everything they type to a big company’s servers to use AI. It’s not quite open source in the strict sense, though, since the licence has restrictions, including on very large companies and on certain uses, so I’d call it “open-ish.” There’s also a real debate about whether releasing powerful models freely is a good idea, with some worried about misuse and others arguing it’s the best way to stop AI being controlled by a handful of giants. On a darker note, Russia hit Odesa’s ports overnight, a day after quitting the grain deal, which feels like a very deliberate message.
