The UK government has formally blamed Chinese state-affiliated hackers for the cyberattack on the Electoral Commission, which gave attackers access to the electoral register details of around 40 million people. Ministers also said a hacking group known as APT31 targeted the emails of a number of MPs and peers, and the UK has sanctioned two individuals and a company linked to it, while the US announced charges against seven people on the same day. China has denied the accusations, calling them groundless. As someone who works in IT, what strikes me is that the attack wasn't some exotic movie hack, but the kind of patient, persistent attack on ordinary systems that every organisation is up against, which is why the boring basics, like patching, MFA and good monitoring, really do matter. With a general election coming this year, I hope this is a wake-up call to take the security of our democratic systems as seriously as the security of our banks. 🔐🗳️
